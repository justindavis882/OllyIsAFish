## 1. Phonetics & Core Rules
* **The Alphabet:** Tesahoda uses exactly 5 vowels (A, E, I, O, U) and 15 consonants (B, D, F, H, J, K, M, N, P, R, S, T, V, W, Y)[cite: 1].
* **The Golden Rule (CV Structure):** Every word must follow a strict Consonant-Vowel (CV) structure[cite: 1]. Consonants must never touch each other, and words must never end in a consonant[cite: 1]. 
* **The Single Exception:** The only exception to the CV rule is the single vowel word "A", which translates to "and"[cite: 1].
* **Proper Names Exemption:** Proper names (such as Olly, Joseph, Olive Oyl, and Zola) bypass all language rules and are spoken exactly as they are in English[cite: 1].

## 2. Sentence Structure & Grammar
* **Word Order:** Sentences strictly follow a Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) order (Actor first, Action second, Target third)[cite: 1].
* **Possession & Modifiers:** Place pronouns or descriptive modifiers directly after the target noun (e.g., *Doma nu* = Our house; *Kobi dako mi* = My black cat)[cite: 1, 17, 18].
* **Plurals:** Indicate more than one of an object by doubling the root word (reduplication) (e.g., *Kobi* → *Kobikobi*)[cite: 2].
* **Negation:** Place the word "Na" immediately before the verb to negate the action[cite: 2].

## 3. The Verb Engine (Time)
Verbs have no irregular forms; time is indicated by adding a two-letter suffix directly to the root action[cite: 1].
* **Present:** Use the root verb (e.g., *Moku* = Eat)[cite: 1].
* **Past:** Add the suffix *-pa* to the root (e.g., *Mokupa* = Ate)[cite: 1].
* **Future:** Add the suffix *-to* to the root (e.g., *Mokuto* = Will eat)[cite: 2].

## 4. Question Formation
Questions are formed "in-situ" without changing the standard sentence order[cite: 2].
* **Yes/No Questions:** Add "Ke" to the very end of the sentence[cite: 2].
* **W-Questions:** Replace the target object in the sentence with the corresponding *Ke-* word: *Kemu* (Who), *Keka* (What), *Kera* (Where), *Keti* (When), *Kemo* (Why), *Kesu* (How)[cite: 2]. 

## 5. Compound Words & Nesting
* **Vocabulary Expansion:** When a specific word is lacking, smash two existing words together (e.g., *Kema-fono* = Game phone/Controller; *Wataduka* = Water room/Bathroom)[cite: 11, 16].
* **Spatial Nesting:** Stack positional terms to pinpoint locations without English prepositions (e.g., *Hina mota* = Here vehicle/In the car; *Tupa tebu* = Top table/On the table)[cite: 20].

* **1-5:** Pa (1), Du (2), Ti (3), Ko (4), Ma (5)[cite: 6].
